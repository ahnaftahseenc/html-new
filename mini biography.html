Mini Biography: Qudrat-I-Khuda

Muhammad Qudrat-i-Khuda was a Bangladeshi chemist, educator, science writer, and public intellectual whose work influenced both scientific research and educational policy in Bangladesh. Best known for his contributions to organic chemistry and his leadership in science education, he played a significant role in shaping the country's post-independence educational vision.

Early Life

Dr. Qudrat-i-Khuda was born on 1 December 1900 in Margram village of Birbhum district, which was then part of British India. From an early age, he was interested in education and scientific subjects. He completed his early education in Bengal and later pursued higher studies in chemistry. He obtained his Master's degree in Chemistry from the University of Calcutta. He then went abroad for further studies and earned a Doctor of Philosophy (PhD) in Chemistry from the University of London.

Career and Scientific Contributions

After completing his higher education, Qudrat-i-Khuda returned to Bengal and began his career as a teacher and researcher. He became a professor of chemistry at the University of Calcutta and conducted research in various areas of chemistry. His research focused on natural products, medicinal plants, food, agriculture, and chemical substances. He and his research team worked on finding useful substances from locally available plants and agricultural products. After the partition of India, he moved to what was then East Pakistan and continued his scientific activities. He played an important role in developing scientific research institutions in the region.

Contribution to Bangladesh

After the independence of Bangladesh in 1971, Dr. Qudrat-i-Khuda continued to contribute to the country's scientific development. He served as the Chairman of the Bangladesh Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (BCSIR). He also led the National Education Commission formed after independence. The commission worked on developing recommendations for improving Bangladesh's education system. His lifelong commitment to scientific research and education helped lay the foundation for scientific development in Bangladesh. Dr. Qudrat-i-Khuda believed that science and education were essential for the progress of a nation. He encouraged young people to develop scientific knowledge and use it for the benefit of society.

Literary and Educational Work

Besides being a scientist, Qudrat-i-Khuda was also an accomplished writer. He wrote and contributed to books and educational materials on science and chemistry. His work helped make scientific knowledge more accessible to Bengali-speaking students and readers.

Scientific Interests

Dr. Qudrat-i-Khuda had a deep interest in chemistry and the study of natural substances. His research included compounds containing elements such as- H2O and CO2 as well as substances used in agriculture and medicine. He believed that scientific knowledge should be used for the betterment of society. His work showed how chemistry could connect with everyday life, from food and plants to agriculture and health. His scientific journey reminds us that curiosity can lead to meaningful discoveries.

Scientific Legacy

Dr. Qudrat-i-Khuda believed that science should be used for the benefit of society. His research in chemistry, agriculture, and natural products made a lasting contribution to scientific development. His work encouraged people to explore the hidden potential of local resources. His dedication to scientific research continues to inspire students who are interested in science and discovery. His contributions remain an important part of Bangladesh's scientific history.

Awards and Recognition

For his outstanding contributions to science and education, Dr. Qudrat-i-Khuda received several honours. He was awarded the Sitara-i-Imtiaz and later received the Ekushey Padak , one of Bangladesh's highest civilian awards.

Death

Dr. Qudrat-i-Khuda died on 3 November 1977 in Dhaka. Although he passed away, his contributions to science, education, and research continue to be remembered in Bangladesh.

Conclusion

Dr. Qudrat-i-Khuda was not only a great scientist but also a dedicated educator and nation-builder. His lifelong commitment to scientific research and education helped lay the foundation for scientific development in Bangladesh. His life teaches us that knowledge, research, and education can play a powerful role in building a better nation.
